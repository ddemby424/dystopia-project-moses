Getting started

RetroEngine is a game maker for retro-style first-person shooters. You draw levels on a top-down grid, drop in enemies, weapons, keys and lights, then press Play to walk through them in 3D. When you're happy, Export turns your project into one HTML file anyone can play in a browser.

  1. Take the first-level tutorial. Menu → Tutorial: build your first level. It takes about 5 minutes.
  2. Or open the Tutorial. Menu → Tutorial (also at tutorial/) teaches adding maps, saving files, putting assets in code, changing engine knobs, and — in the advanced lessons — writing scripts and calling APIs.
  3. Or open a starter level. The level picker in the top bar has three finished levels (E1M1–E1M3). Play them, then take them apart to see how they work.
  4. Save often. Work autosaves in this browser. Use Menu → Projects for named saves, and Export project file (.json) for real backups.

Tutorial

Menu → Tutorial opens the Tinkerer tutorial: adding maps, saving and sharing files, loading assets in code, and changing engine knobs, with an advanced section on level scripts and APIs. You can also open tutorial/tutorial.html (or just tutorial/) directly. For a five-minute first level inside the editor, use Menu → Tutorial: build your first level.

The editor screen

  • Top bar: project name, level picker, undo/redo, Play, Export and the Menu.
  • Explorer (left on tablets and desktop; a slide-out sheet on phones, opened with the folder button): the project name, the levels list, custom models, and level scripts.
  • Tool bar (left on tablets and desktop, bottom on phones): the drawing tools.
  • Map: one finger or the mouse uses the current tool. Two fingers pan and pinch-zoom; on desktop use Space+drag and the mouse wheel. Fit frames the whole level.
  • Properties / inspector (top of the right panel): dropdowns for the wall, floor, ceiling, height, door type and light level of the hovered cell. If you drag a rectangle with the Select tool, the same dropdowns apply to every cell in that selection.
  • Panel tabs (right; a slide-up sheet on folded phones, opened with the panel button): Build (texture swatches, door types, heights), Things (enemies, items, props, lights), Rooms (template library), Level (sky, fog, lighting presets, music, script) and Project (levels, models, map layers).
  • Status bar (bottom on desktop and tablets; a floating readout on phones): the grid coordinates under the cursor or finger, plus a readable description of what is on that cell — wall or floor texture, height, door type, thing name (for example Soldier or Exit pad), light level and the current map layer.

Building good levels

  • Start with rooms. The Rooms tab has 24 rotatable templates: rooms, corridors, stairs and buildings such as Bunker, Guard Tower, Mezzanine, Courtyard, Hangar and Server Room, plus Clean Lab and Med Bay for well-lit white rooms. Tap one, tap the map, and press R or ⟳ to rotate.
  • Connect rooms with doors. Use a plain door for flow and keycard doors to gate progress. Put the key somewhere the player must fight through.
  • Use height. Catwalks and raised platforms give enemies firing positions, and stairs let the player reach them.
  • Light it. Coloured light fixtures (Things → Light) set the mood. Flickering lights make horror corners. The Level tab has a lighting preset: Gritty for dark industrial maps, Clean bright for labs and offices, and Emergency red for an alarm wash. For a clean well-lit room, stamp Clean Lab or Med Bay, pick the Clean bright preset, and use Lab Wall Panels, Polished Tile and Bright Ceiling Lights from the Properties dropdowns.
  • Give cover. Crates, sandbags and barrels block bullets. The smarter enemies hide behind them, and barrels explode.
  • Check it. Menu → Check level confirms the exit can be reached and that keys come before their doors.

Enemies, items and props

EnemyBehaviour
SoldierFast rifleman. Strafes while reloading, flanks, and calls in nearby allies.
Gun DroneHovering, fast and fragile. Hard to hit.
Heavy WalkerSlow armoured walker that fires 3-shot bursts.
Sentry TurretNever moves. Rapid fire with long sight lines.
Titan (Boss)Huge HP and twin rockets. Explodes when it dies.

How enemies think: they stand idle until they see you (line of sight, roughly in front of them) or hear you (gunfire carries far; running footsteps carry a short way). An alerted enemy radios nearby allies. In a fight they strafe between shots, some swing wide to flank, and badly hurt ones run to cover and pop back out. They path around walls and open plain doors themselves. Difficulty (Easy / Normal / Hard, in game Settings) changes their health, damage, fire rate, speed and reaction time.

Pickups: health, medkit, armor, ammo (bullets, shells, cells, rockets), weapon pickups (shotgun, rifle, plasma, rockets; you start with the wrench and pistol), the three keycards, and data chips (secrets). Props: crates, barrels (explosive), lockers, server racks, metal shelves, terminals, desks, pillars, pipes, generators, sandbags, lamps, cones, debris, control consoles, lab benches, med cabinets, chem vats, industrial fans, vents and extinguishers. Most of these use real CC0 GLB models by Quaternius (Sci-Fi Essentials Kit and Modular Sci-Fi MegaKit); a procedural stand-in is used if a model fails to load, and for the med cabinet, extinguisher, generator and floor lamp.

Level settings & music

The Level tab sets the name, sky (Night City, Red Dusk, Overcast, Toxic Haze, Black Void), fog, ambient light, wall height, default textures, par time and music. Use the Lighting preset dropdown for a one-tap mood: Gritty, Clean bright, or Emergency red. You can still tweak ambient light and fog after picking a preset.

To paint floors faster, use the Properties dropdowns at the top of the right panel (wall, floor, ceiling, height, door, light level). They edit the hovered cell, or every cell in a Select-tool rectangle.

Music: pick Playlist to shuffle the bundled tracks, a single track, or Synth ambient, the built-in generative music that works offline. Ten CC BY 4.0 tracks ship with the editor (metal, industrial, dark synth, trap, hip-hop). During play, open Pause → Music player to switch tracks and see their credits. To add your own music, put MP3s in assets/music/ and list them in MUSIC_TRACKS in dmbtech1.js. Only use music you have the rights to (CC0 or CC BY), never someone else's songs without a license, and credit it.

Level scripting

Menu → Code editor → script.js. Scripts react to events: start, tick(dt), kill(type), pickup(type), door, use({x,y}) and exit.

api.on('start', () => api.message('Find the red keycard.'));
api.on('kill', () => { if (api.enemiesLeft() === 0) { api.message('Vault unlocked'); api.openDoor(20, 8); } });
api.on('use', c => { if (c.x === 5 && c.y === 9) api.giveAmmo('rockets', 2); });

API: message(t), heal(n), giveArmor(n), giveAmmo(type, n), openDoor(x, y), enemiesLeft(), kills(), time(), win(), sound(name) and player {x, y, hp, armor, keys}.

Saving, exporting & publishing

  • Projects: Menu → Projects keeps named save slots in this browser.
  • Backups: Export project file (.json) and Import project file. Old DOOMFORGE projects and saves are imported automatically.
  • Export game: makes one .html file with your levels. The 3D models and music load from where RetroEngine is hosted. Offline, the game falls back to built-in models and synth music, so it always works.
  • Publish: put the whole retroengine/ folder (with assets/) on any static host, such as GitHub Pages, Netlify or itch.io (HTML5), and share the link.

Sharing your maps

Send a single level to a friend in one of two ways. Find both in the Level tab under Share this level, or in the Menu.

  • Share link: the whole level is compressed into the link itself (the part after #map=), so no server or account is needed. On a phone, the share sheet opens (Messages, WhatsApp, Discord…). On a computer, the link is copied. Opening the link loads the level and drops you straight into Playtest. Press Back to editor to look at how it was built, and it is added to your project as a new level.
  • Export .retro file: a small file holding the level plus any custom models (including uploaded .glb files). Use it when a level is too big for a link (about 7 KB). RetroEngine tells you when that happens. To open one, use Menu → Import level (.retro), or drag the file onto the editor. On phones you pick it from Files, Drive or Downloads.

Safety: level scripts from other people are switched off when you import. The Level tab shows a warning, and you can review the code in Edit level script and press Apply to turn it on. Imported files are checked for size, map dimensions and valid entities. A whole project .json also works with Import level.

Tip: links only work for others when RetroEngine is hosted (for example on GitHub Pages). A copy opened from a file on your own device makes links that only work there.

Phones & tablets

  • Works in Chrome, Samsung Internet, Firefox, Safari (iPhone/iPad) and in-app browsers. Everything is touch-first: no mouse, keyboard or pointer lock needed.
  • Editor: one finger uses the tool, two fingers pan and pinch-zoom the map. The Panel button opens the side panel as a sheet.
  • Game: use the Full screen button (where the browser supports it) to hide the address bar. Turn the phone sideways for the best view. Sound starts on your first tap (a phone rule).
  • Graphics quality: Auto (the default) picks settings for your device and lowers the resolution on the fly to keep the game smooth. Choose Low/Medium/High in Settings to fix it.
  • If a phone can't run 3D (no WebGL), Playtest explains why and the editor keeps working. Try another browser, or turn off battery saver.

Custom 3D models

Project → Custom 3D models lets you replace any enemy or prop with your own .glb file or URL. Models are auto-scaled and placed on the floor. Animated models use clips named Idle, Walk/Run, Shoot/Attack, HitReact and Death. Use only CC0 or CC-BY models, credit the authors, and keep files under about 2 MB because uploads are stored in the project.

Troubleshooting

  • Black screen or "needs Three.js": the 3D engine loads from a CDN the first time, so connect to the internet once.
  • Enemies look like simple robots and the music is synth: the asset pack couldn't load. That happens when the page is opened straight from a file (file://) with no internet. Open it through a web server, or from the hosted link.
  • Mouse look doesn't lock: some in-app browsers block pointer lock. Drag to look instead.
  • Slow on a phone: keep Graphics quality on Auto, or choose Low. Close other apps, and turn off battery saver (it limits the frame rate).
  • Share link does nothing: some in-app browsers block the share sheet and clipboard. Select the link text in the box and copy it, or export a .retro file.
  • "3D not available": the browser has WebGL turned off or blocked. Open the page in a full browser (not an in-app one) and enable hardware acceleration.
  • Storage full: browser storage is about 5–10 MB. Remove uploaded models, or export a JSON backup and start a new project.
